Network

Read the traffic. Then break it.

An HTTP and HTTPS inspector in the spirit of Proxyman and HTTP Toolkit, built into the window of the device it watches.

  • HTTPS, decrypted. Deckhand puts its certificate in the system store of a rootable emulator, in the simulator's keychain, or walks you through a phone.
  • An Android helper that sets itself up. A tiny VPN app points the device at the proxy while you capture, and lets go when you stop.
  • Search, filter, copy as cURL. Headers, bodies, JSON, images, timing, and a per-device filter it remembers.
  • Slow networks. Wi-Fi, LTE, 3G, Edge, a very bad network or none at all.

Logs

Logcat that you can actually read.

Search by text or regex, filter by level and by app, arrange the columns, and copy exactly the lines you selected. Nothing is read from a device until the panel is open.

  • Per-app filters with your own apps first, and a button for the app in the foreground.
  • Follows the end until you scroll up, and stays where you are.
  • Remembers each device across launches.

And everything around it

The small things that take a minute each.

Deckhand is a menu bar app first. The quick actions are one click away, and the window opens when you want more.

Start headless

Boot an emulator without its window, or a simulator without the Simulator app. The mirror is the only screen.

Real shortcuts

⇧⌘H for Home, ⇧⌘A for dark mode, ⌘L to lock: the Simulator's own keys, on Android too.

Install anything

.app, .apk, .aab, .ipa, a .tar.gz from EAS, or a link. Drop a file on the window and it goes to the right place.

Install links

expo-orbit:// and deckhand:// links download a build and install it on the device you pick.

Virtual devices

Create, rename, clone and delete simulators and AVDs, with the advanced hardware options, without opening Xcode or Android Studio.

Virtual camera

Give a simulator a camera: your webcam, a still or a looping video, for scanners and photo capture.

Appearance and text

Dark mode, text size, clean 9:41 status bar, animations, show taps and location in a click.

Physical devices

Android phones over adb and iPhones over devicectl, next to your simulators: install, apps, screenshots, mirror.

Swift 6 and Xcode. The helper app is optional and needs a JDK.

# clone
git clone https://github.com/ossappscollective/deckhand.git
cd deckhand

# optional: the Android helper app (JDK 17 or 21, Android SDK 36)
DeckhandHelper/build.sh

# build and run
xcodebuild -project Deckhand.xcodeproj -scheme Deckhand -derivedDataPath build build
open build/Build/Products/Debug/Deckhand.app
Questions

Before you ask.

Does it work without Xcode, or without Android Studio?

Yes. Each side works without the other. With no Xcode the iOS section shows a hint and Android keeps working, and the other way round. Only the Android SDK's emulator and platform-tools are needed for Android.

Why can't it decrypt some apps' HTTPS?

Android apps that target Android 7 or later ignore certificates the user installs, so only the system certificate store works for them. That needs an emulator image that allows adb root (AOSP or Google APIs, not Google Play). Deckhand reads the device and says so, and a banner offers to trust the certificate system-wide when the image allows it. Apps that pin their certificates cannot be decrypted by any proxy.

Does it phone home?

No. Deckhand makes no network requests of its own. The only download is the one you trigger yourself, such as installing a build from a URL.

Is there a Windows or Linux version?

Not yet. Deckhand is a native macOS app. An Android-only build for Windows and Linux is being investigated.

How is it related to BootBar?

Deckhand began as a fork of BootBar by Dipendra Sharma and keeps its MIT license. It has grown a lot since then, and has its own name.
